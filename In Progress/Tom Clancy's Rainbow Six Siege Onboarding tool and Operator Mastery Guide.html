Use this as a flexible starting path, not a set of mandatory rules. Adjust recommendations to your comfort and the operators you enjoy playing.

These are starting recommendations, not universal rules. Optics are personal preference: use Magnified A when available, or Holo B for a 1.0x optic. Shotguns use Holo B in this guide. No lasers are recommended because the visible beam can reveal your position while holding an angle.

Calibrate Sensitivity and Recoil

  1. Start in the Shooting Range after setting up your preferred attachments.
  2. Use the F2 as a challenging baseline for recoil practice; it is a reference, not a requirement.
  3. Adjust sensitivity gradually and test one change at a time.
  4. Try the settings in Deathmatch to see whether they work in live fights.

AI feedback from a clip and your settings can be an optional troubleshooting aid, not a required step or a definitive prescription.

Recommended Training Modes

  • Clear House: Learn map layouts and room connections.
  • Deathmatch: Practice recoil control in live engagements.
  • Field Training: Practice site setup and teamwork.

These modes are available under Training in the Playlists menu.

Beginner-Friendly Operators to Try

Attack: Sledge introduces soft breaching and gadget destruction; IQ helps locate electronics; Thermite teaches hard breaching; Thatcher supports hard breaches with EMPs.

Defense: Rook offers simple, place-and-forget armor; Frost teaches awareness of entry routes with her Welcome Mats; Vigil is an approachable example of roaming and wasting attacker time.

Roles & Glossary

Ubisoft's operator-select labels describe intended functions. Community roles describe how an operator or strategy is commonly used in a round; the operator cards connect those ideas to practical play.

6 / “I've got your six”

“Six” means behind or to the rear. “I've got your six” means a teammate is watching the area behind you, often a flank route.

90

An L-shaped hallway or corner callout. The exact location depends on the map.

Anchor

A defender who stays near site to hold important routes, protect the objective, and support teammates with information.

Anti-Gadget

An operator role focused on finding, disabling, or destroying enemy gadgets.

Anti-Entry

Utility or positioning that obstructs routes, punishes unchecked entries, or helps deny access to site.

Area Denial

Utility that makes an area dangerous or difficult to occupy, helping delay or redirect a push.

CC / Crowd Control

Tools that impair vision, movement, or effectiveness to control an area or slow a push. CC is also an Ubisoft operator-select label.

Crossfire

Two or more players covering different angles on the same area so an opponent cannot safely challenge one without being exposed to another.

EMP (Electromagnetic Pulse)

An effect that disrupts electronic gadgets within its range, including Bandit Batteries, Kaid Electroclaws, Kapkan Traps, and Alibi Prismas. It can also temporarily remove the aiming reticle from an operator's optic if they are caught in the blast radius.

Entry Fragger / Front Line

An attacker who takes space at the front of a push and helps create the first opportunities to engage defenders.

Flank Watch

Watching routes attackers may use to approach from behind or from the side of the main push.

Hard Breach

Opening reinforced walls or hatches to create new entry points and pressure defenders.

Intel

Information gathered from cameras, gadgets, sound, or observation. Intel is also an Ubisoft operator-select label.

Intel Denial

Preventing opponents from gathering or using information, such as by disabling cameras or interfering with drones.

Last Known Position

The most recent location where an opponent was seen or reliably detected.

Lurk / Lurker

A player who stays near the main group or in an adjacent area to control space, gather information, or punish rotations.

Map Control

Control of rooms and routes that let a team move safely, deny rotations, and apply pressure.

OBJ

Short for objective: the bomb site or area the team must attack or defend.

Plant

Planting the defuser at a bomb site to begin the attacker-side post-plant phase.

Power Position

A position that provides a strong view or control over important routes while limiting exposure.

Roam / Roamer

A defender playing away from site to gather information, threaten attackers, and consume their time before returning or taking a fight.

Shallow Roam / Deep Roam

A shallow roam stays close enough to site to rotate back quickly. A deep roam operates farther away and should still account for site pressure and the time needed to return.

Soft Breach

Opening destructible, unreinforced surfaces to create lines of sight, routes, or vertical pressure.

Utility

Gadgets, abilities, and other resources operators bring to help the team.

Utility Removal / Gadget Destruction

Destroying or disabling enemy utility so it cannot affect the round.

Utility Sink

An attacker or strategy that pressures defenders into spending utility, time, or other resources to stop the push, leaving fewer resources to defend against the rest of the team.

Vertical Play

Pressuring players or utility through destructible floors or ceilings from another level.

Yellow Ping

A contextual ping that marks a location for teammates without necessarily identifying an opponent.

Red Ping

A ping that identifies an opponent's location when the game's spotting rules allow it.

RFF

Reverse Friendly Fire, a system that penalizes friendly fire to discourage harmful team damage.

Trade / Trade Kill

Eliminating an opponent immediately after they eliminate a teammate, so the team does not lose the engagement without a response.

Core Gameplay

Choose for the Round

Consider what the team needs and what the site calls for, but also choose an operator you are comfortable using. If you want to learn an unfamiliar role, try it in a lower-pressure mode; in a higher-stakes match, a familiar pick can help you contribute confidently.

Play to Your Strengths

Use your operator's ability and your own strengths to help the team. Mechanical skill matters, but it is not the only way to contribute.

Attack Together and Stay Objective-Focused

Move with your team and avoid spending too long clearing roamers. Isolated fights can waste time and leave you exposed. If challenged, reposition or regroup. When a teammate takes a fight, look for a different angle rather than following into the same firing lane.

Roam with a Purpose

Roaming can delay attackers and create pressure away from site, but watch the site situation and leave enough time to rotate back. Waiting until anchors are already overwhelmed can make recovery difficult.

Anchor with Intention

Hold important routes, listen for pushes, and use cameras to support teammates. If opponents identify or challenge your position, change your angle or relocate instead of repeatedly exposing yourself from the same spot.

Create Value Beyond Kills

Intel, gadget destruction, area denial, breaching, healing, flank watch, crowd control, and wasting attacker time can all help win a round. A player can make a meaningful contribution without getting an elimination.

Weapon Setup and Recoil

Attachment choices are personal. This guide prioritizes controllable recoil—especially horizontal recoil—while balancing rate of fire, damage, and handling. Sensitivity is individual; higher is not automatically better. Make adjustments gradually and test them in the Shooting Range and Deathmatch. Lasers can reveal your position when holding an angle.

Attackers

Sledge2-Speed / 2-Armor · Difficulty 1/3

Ubisoft labels: Breach, Anti-Gadget
Expected roles: Soft Breach, Gadget Destruction

Basic Use

Sledge's hammer can open soft walls, hatches, and floors around or above site. It can also destroy barbed wire, deployable shields, and Castle barricades with one swing. Because the hammer is non-electronic, jamming signals will not disable it.

Advanced Play: Vertical Play

Use the Caber to open soft floors and pressure the area below. Its durability allows multiple openings, but Sledge must get close to the floor, which can expose him to danger.

Loadout 1

Primary: L85A2
Secondary: Reaper MK2 or P226
Secondary gadget: Frag Grenades or Stun Grenades

Loadout 2

Primary: M590A1
Secondary: Reaper MK2
Secondary gadget: Frag Grenades or Stun Grenades

Thatcher1-Speed / 3-Armor · Difficulty 2/3

Ubisoft labels: Anti-Gadget, Support
Expected role: EMP

Basic Use

Thatcher should usually support the team's hard breacher. His EMPs temporarily disrupt Bandit Batteries, Kaid Electroclaws, and Mute Jammers, helping the hard breacher open reinforced walls or hatches.

Advanced Play

If Bandit or Kaid is tricking—or Tubarão is delaying the breach—watch the disruption timers on affected gadgets that remain intact. Use another EMP just before the effect wears off to refresh the disruption and give the hard breacher more time.

Synergy

Thatcher works well with Thermite, Hibana, and Ace, as well as operators carrying secondary hard-breach gadgets. His EMPs can help clear electronic denial so they can breach.

Loadout

Primary: L85A2, AR33, PMR90A2, or M590A1
Secondary: P226 Mk 25
Secondary gadget: Claymore or Breach Charges

Ash3-Speed / 1-Armor · Difficulty 2/3

Ubisoft labels: Breach, Front Line
Expected roles: Utility Destruction, Utility Sink, Entry Fragger

Basic Use

Use Ash's breaching rounds to open soft walls and hatches or destroy deployable shields. She can also take an aggressive entry-fragger role, leading pushes.

Advanced Play: Vertical Gadget Destruction

Ash can breach soft floors to destroy defender utility above and potentially damage defenders. On Kanal, if the objective is 2F Radar / 2F Server Room and Bandit Batteries are blocking the exterior breach on the 2F Renovations wall, Ash can move to 1F Security Room and breach the floor beneath them. This can destroy the batteries and may damage Bandit if he is trying to trick the wall.

Her breaching rounds can also destroy Maestro's Evil Eyes.

Counter-Play

Ash can destroy Castle barricades with her breaching rounds. Pairing Castle with Kaid or Mute can prevent the round from detonating, leaving the barricade in place. Jäger and Wamai can intercept her rounds. If Bandit electrifies barbed wire, Ash's breaching round will be destroyed if fired at the wire; she must shoot the battery with her gun first, then breach the wire.

Loadout

Primary: R4-C or G36C
Secondary: 5.7 USG or M45 MEUSOC
Secondary gadget: Claymore or Breach Charges

Thermite2-Speed / 2-Armor · Difficulty 1/3

Ubisoft label: Breach Support
Expected role: Hard Breach

Basic Use

Thermite can breach reinforced walls and hatches when anti-breach utility is not stopping his Exothermic Charges. This includes Bandit Batteries, Kaid Electroclaws, Mute Jammers, and Tubarão Zoto Canisters. His main target should usually be an exterior site wall: opening it applies pressure and creates options for the team's push.

Advanced Play: Coordinating Through Anti-Breach

When anti-breach utility is present, Thermite needs to coordinate with teammates to clear or disable it. Timing and communication are important. On applicable sites, IQ can use her scanner to locate electronic gadgets and shoot them through floors or ceilings. Thatcher, Twitch, and Kali can also help disable or remove anti-breach utility.

If a Zoto Canister is affecting a placed charge, leave the charge in place; it can be activated once the anti-breach is destroyed or disabled and the freeze no longer prevents detonation.

Synergy

Thermite pairs well with Thatcher, Twitch, Kali, and IQ. Their utility or gadget-clearing plays help him get an Exothermic Charge through anti-breach denial.

Counter-Play

Hard counters: Bandit, Kaid, and Mute can prevent his charges from opening reinforced surfaces.
Soft counter: Tubarão can delay the breach with Zoto Canisters.

Loadout

Primary: 556XI
Secondary: ITA12S
Secondary gadget: Smoke Grenades or Stun Grenades

Twitch2-Speed / 2-Armor · Difficulty 2/3

Ubisoft labels: Anti-Gadget, Intel
Expected roles: Gadget Destruction, Utility Sink

Basic Use

Twitch's drone can destroy or disable defender utility. Common targets include:

  • Static cameras; Kapkan Traps; Thorn's Razorbloom Shells; Ela's Grzmot Mines; Mozzie's Pests.
  • Bandit Batteries; Kaid Electroclaws; Mute Jammers; Jäger's ADS; Wamai's MAG-NETs.
  • Thunderbird's Kona Stations; Valkyrie's Black Eyes; Fenrir's F-NATT Dread Mines; Alibi's Prismas; Denari's T.R.I.P. Connectors.

Her drone can also act as a utility sink: defenders may spend impacts or time chasing it to destroy it, diverting resources and attention.

Advanced Play: Additional Targets

Look for Mira's Black Mirror gas canister, Aruni's Surya Gates, Echo's Yokai Drones, Maestro's Evil Eyes when open, Lesion's GU Mines, Melusi's Banshees when active, and Goyo's Canisters.

Synergy

Twitch works well with Thermite, Hibana, and Ace, as well as operators carrying secondary hard-breach gadgets. Her drone can help clear anti-breach utility so they can open reinforced surfaces.

Counter-Play

Mute is Twitch's only identified hard counter; his jammers can disable her drones.
